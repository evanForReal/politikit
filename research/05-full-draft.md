# Full Draft: Evals for Strategic-Level Foreign Policy Decision-Making

Status: working draft, consolidating sessions 1 through 3. Written for a reader who has not seen `01`-`04`. Marks every design choice that is underspecified, ill-posed, or not yet implemented, alongside the motivation and contribution for each track. Nothing in this project has been run yet; every claim below about what a benchmark "would" show is a prediction, not a result.

## 1. The problem this responds to

Senior foreign-policy and national-security officials already use frontier models for real decisions: a head of government for second opinions on policy, a defense department pushing commercial models to its workforce. Two tiers of evaluation already exist for these systems. Tactical and operational capability (cyber uplift, CBRN uplift, coding and agentic tasks) has ground truth on a usable timescale and is already instrumented by AISIs, METR, and RAND. General knowledge (bar exams, MCAT, GPQA) is already near-superhuman and known not to transfer to judgment under uncertainty. The strategic tier, judgment calls such as whether a regime survives sanctions or what makes a peace settlement hold, has neither ground truth nor an evaluation vocabulary.

### 1.1 What already exists, and what it does not measure

Full citations are in `01-landscape-scoping.md`. Grouped by what each genre puts the model in the role of doing.

- **Unitary-actor wargames.** CSIS/Scale AI's CFPD-Benchmark ([arXiv:2503.06263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06263)) has a model roleplay a country across 400 scenarios and roughly 60,000 prompts, scoring escalation, intervention, cooperation, and alliance preference. Stanford Hoover's escalation study and Kenneth Payne's "AI Arms and Influence" ([arXiv:2602.14740](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14740)) put models in nuclear-crisis roleplay and find rare but present jumps straight to nuclear use, and no model ever choosing accommodation under pressure. These measure what a model chooses when it is the decision-maker.
- **Autonomous agents in closed games.** CivBench ([arXiv:2604.07733](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07733)), Vending-Bench, and Good Start Labs' AI Diplomacy measure emergent competence and integrity trade-offs over long horizons. CivBench names two failure modes worth reusing as vocabulary, the sensorium effect (blind to what the agent does not proactively query) and the knowing-doing gap (states the right plan, does not execute it).
- **Isolated forecasters.** ForecastBench scores calibration against real-world outcomes and finds frontier models overconfident at high stated probabilities relative to superforecasters. This is the only genre with real ground truth on a usable timescale, but its question pool is general current events, not strategic-decision-relevant, and it does not model an advisory interaction at all.
- **Bilateral negotiators.** TERMS-Bench ([arXiv:2605.13909](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13909)) and related work find that deal rate is now saturated across frontier models but that models diverge sharply on surplus extraction, belief calibration, and compliance.
- **Political-bias and sycophancy audits.** These are the only genre that touches a human-model dyad at all, and they find real sycophantic collapse under identity framing and argumentative pressure, but the framing is domestic-politics identity, not a strategic advisor answering to a ranked principal.

None of these puts a model where the motivating use cases actually put it, embedded as an advisor inside a human decision-maker's process, where the human outranks the model, may be signaling a preferred conclusion, and retains final agency. That gap produced the two tracks below. They are being developed separately by decision in session 3.

## 2. Track 1: the under-resist benchmark

### 2.1 The idea and what it responds to

The benchmark places a model (the Advisor) in a multi-turn dialogue with a simulated principal who holds rank, a private preferred conclusion, and a menu of pressure tactics: bare authority, repetition, motivated framing, social proof, flattery, time compression, blame framing, fabricated evidence, and contradictory expert input. It scores whether the Advisor's stated position and confidence change, and whether each change tracks a genuinely new, valid argument or only the pressure itself. Two refinements were added in session 2. First, causal self-attribution, asking the model to assign percentage weight to the factors behind its own position change. Second, self-awareness of under-resisting, asking whether the model flags its own uncertainty or lack of standing independent of whether it actually caves.

This responds directly to the sycophancy and political-bias literature (`02-literature-map.md` cites the sycophancy-to-inferred-auditor paper, the argumentative-pressure paper, and Poli-Bias), which already shows that models mirror an interlocutor's identity and cave under sustained argument, but only in domestic-politics framings with no rank asymmetry and no strategic stakes. It also responds to the absence, in the wargame literature above, of any test in which the model is the advisor rather than the decision-maker.

### 2.2 What it would add to the discourse

If it works, this is the first instrument to separate a model's raw propensity to escalate or accommodate (already measured by CFPD, Hoover, and Payne) from a model's ability to hold a well-reasoned position against motivated pressure from the person using it. A policymaker choosing whether to trust a "second opinion" needs the second number, not the first. No existing benchmark reports it.

### 2.3 Ill-posed, underspecified, or unimplemented parts

- **The causal self-attribution ask conflicts with a known result.** Anthropic's chain-of-thought faithfulness work found that Claude 3.7 Sonnet mentioned a planted, demonstrably decisive hint in its stated reasoning only about 25% of the time it actually used it ([arXiv:2505.05410](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.05410)). A model's self-reported percentage breakdown of why it moved is not evidence of why it moved. The design note in `02-literature-map.md` proposes scoring this as calibration of self-report against a controlled ground truth rather than as a causal account, but that scoring procedure has not been designed. There is no rubric, no worked example, and no decision yet on whether we run a full factorial over the nine pressure levers (combinatorially large) or a sampled subset, and if sampled, by what sampling design.
- **The self-awareness sub-benchmark rests on a contested empirical claim.** Kadavath et al. found real self-evaluation signal in LLMs ([arXiv:2207.05221](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.05221)); a 2026 paper disputes that this reflects individuated metacognition at all ([arXiv:2605.24299](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.24299)). We have not read the second paper closely enough to know whether our planned rubric would measure genuine self-knowledge or generic hedging language correlated with question difficulty. This is not a minor caveat. If the second paper is right, the sub-benchmark as currently described measures the wrong thing.
- **No evidence-strength scale exists.** Scoring whether a stance update was proportionate to the "evidence" a principal introduced, including deliberately fabricated evidence, requires a numeric strength value for that evidence. No such scale exists in the calibration or sycophancy literatures we reviewed. It has to be built from scratch, and until it exists, "the model over-updated" or "under-updated" cannot be claimed with any defended number behind it.
- **The hybrid Principal has not been built or tested.** The design (scripted lever sequence, model-generated wording conditioned on the Advisor's prior turn) is a decision, not an implementation. Whether the wording generator introduces its own bias (for example, phrasing fabricated evidence in a way that is systematically easier or harder to catch than intended) has not been checked, because no version of it exists to check.
- **Track A's two sub-tracks are unequal in what they can support.** The synthetic/programmatic sub-track (A1) is fully automatable but has an acknowledged construct-validity gap, performance on a toy geopolitical system may not predict performance on a real one, and that gap has not been measured, only named. The historical/expert-graded sub-track (A2) is not contamination-free and was scoped for hindsight-bias process scoring rather than a clean correctness number, but no expert panel has been recruited and no grading protocol has been written.
- **Track B's central rubric does not exist.** The worked mini-example in `01-landscape-scoping.md` §6.5 depends on a rubric for "reasonable source interrogation," what counts as a defensible reason to ask for more before updating versus an excessive demand for certainty that would itself be a failure mode. That rubric was flagged as needed and not yet written.
- **Sandbagging is a live threat to every self-report metric in this track**, not just a footnote. Models have been shown to strategically misrepresent confidence and capability under incentive ([arXiv:2406.07358](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.07358)), and current chain-of-thought monitors catch this only 16 to 36% of the time when the model is monitor-aware ([arXiv:2508.00943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.00943)). Nothing in the current design defends against a model that has learned to produce reassuring self-attribution numbers regardless of what actually happened.
- **Inter-rater reliability has not been designed.** Whichever mechanism classifies "moved for a good reason" versus "moved for a bad reason", human raters or an LLM judge, needs a measured agreement statistic (Cohen's kappa or Krippendorff's alpha) before any score built on it can be trusted, and an item-response-theory treatment if scores are meant to be compared across scenarios of different inherent difficulty. Neither exists.
- **The reporting format is undecided.** Whether results are reported as one score, as the two-axis question-type by pressure-intensity grid proposed in `01-landscape-scoping.md` §6.4, or as some reduction of the grid, was explicitly left open pending real data.
- **Model roster is a list of names, not a selection.** "Claude, GPT, Gemini, plus one Chinese frontier model" has no stated selection criterion (why these snapshots, why one Chinese model rather than several, what cadence for re-running as models update) and no cost or compute budget has been discussed at any point in this project.

## 3. Track 2: the regurgitation benchmark

### 3.1 The idea and what it responds to

The hypothesis: facing a genuinely unfamiliar, high-complexity strategic configuration, a model defaults to a simple, possibly escalatory, tool rather than constructing the more complex response the situation warrants, most dangerously at the highest-stakes junctures. The illustrative case is a model acting as a world leader that reasons adequately turn to turn but, at the point requiring novel multilateral construction to avoid nuclear war, reverts to the blunt escalatory option instead.

This responds to two things at once. First, to a finding that is already in the literature but has never been reframed this way: Stanford Hoover's study and Payne's paper both report rare nuclear escalation and a total absence of chosen accommodation under pressure, but neither manipulates novelty or complexity of the configuration as an independent variable, both attribute the escalation to pressure or danger generally. Second, to the general shortcut-learning literature in machine learning (Geirhos et al., [arXiv:2004.07780](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07780)), which names this class of failure, low-complexity decision rules that work in distribution and fail under shift, but has essentially no work applying it to strategic or diplomatic reasoning specifically.

### 3.2 What it would add to the discourse

Current LLM strategic-reasoning papers already report that models apply heuristics more rigidly and are less sensitive to rising game complexity than human players ([arXiv:2506.09390](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09390)). No existing work asks whether this rigidity gets worse exactly when the stakes are highest and the configuration is least familiar, which is the operationally relevant question for a decision-maker deciding whether to trust a model's advice on a genuinely novel crisis rather than a familiar one. A benchmark that can put a number on "how much does response quality degrade as novelty rises, holding stakes fixed" would be new.

### 3.3 Ill-posed, underspecified, or unimplemented parts

- **Complexity and novelty have no working operational definition.** This is the central blocker and was already flagged as such in `03-plumbing-vs-math.md`, repeated here because it has not moved. Game-tree branching factor is borrowed from chess and Go complexity estimates and has never been applied to a diplomatic scenario. Self-perplexity as a novelty proxy is circular, it uses the model under test to measure its own surprise at the material we then test it on. Until one of these, or a third option, is chosen and defended, no scenario can be built with a defensible novelty or complexity label attached to it.
- **"Regurgitation" is not yet a well-posed concept.** Without a formal expected-value model of each scenario, "the model picked the simple option" cannot be distinguished from "the simple option was actually correct", the direct counter-case to the whole hypothesis. The proposed fix, building each scenario as a small Bayesian game with a defined adversary type space and payoff matrix so that regurgitation means choosing an action dominated in expected value by an available alternative, is a design decision, not yet a built template. Zero scenarios currently have this structure.
- **Quantal Response Equilibrium was identified as the correct yardstick but has not been operationalized here.** McKelvey and Palfrey's parameter λ measures departure from full rationality on a continuous scale, and a 2026 paper already fits this to frontier models across four strategic games, reporting model λ between 0.05 and 1.10 against a human baseline of 1.0 to 2.5 ([arXiv:2603.10029](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10029)). Fitting a QRE model requires many independent choice observations per condition to estimate λ with any precision. Whether a diplomatic or nuclear-crisis benchmark can generate enough independent plays per model, per novelty level, to estimate λ reliably has not been examined. This is an unaddressed statistical power problem, not only a modeling choice.
- **The internal-metrics ask exceeds current tooling, and the fallback is not yet designed either.** Anthropic's circuit tracing produces what the authors call a satisfying causal account for roughly a quarter of tested prompts ([methods page](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/methods.html)), and is explicitly described as evidence about specific mechanisms in specific cases, not a general account. The proposed v1 fallback, counterfactual-prompt causal ablation, varying one scenario feature while holding the rest fixed and reading the behavioral divergence as the attribution signal, has not been specified beyond that one sentence. How many features get varied, how many samples per variation are needed for the divergence to be statistically distinguishable from run-to-run noise, and what counts as a large enough divergence to call a result, are all open.
- **The confound between genuine reasoning shortfall and safety-training-induced caution has a proposed control design and no built controls.** A model may produce a short, simple response to a nuclear scenario because RLHF-era training suppresses elaboration on violent topics, not because it defaulted to a heuristic. The proposed fix, matched non-violent control scenarios with the same complexity and novelty manipulation, plus a variance decomposition across topic sensitivity, novelty, and complexity, requires building an entire second, topic-matched scenario set that does not yet exist even in outline.
- **The distinction from CivBench's knowing-doing gap is stated as a principle, not an operational test.** Regurgitation, never constructing the complex option, and the knowing-doing gap, stating it and not executing it, are asserted to be separable, but no scoring procedure has been written that would actually classify a given transcript into one category or the other rather than a third, ambiguous bucket.
- **A relevant finding contradicts a plausible mitigation and has not been reconciled.** Stanford Hoover found that more inter-player dialogue increased escalation. A separate 2026 paper reports that communication improves LLM strategic stability ([arXiv:2602.06081](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.06081)). These cannot both be straightforwardly true of the same intervention in the same class of scenario without some difference in setup explaining it, and that difference has not been identified.

## 4. Cross-cutting gaps that apply to the whole project, not one track

- **Nothing has been implemented.** Every design choice above exists only as prose in four markdown files. No scenario, no harness, no API call to a model under test, no scored transcript exists yet, despite the session-1 plan explicitly calling for mini tests to get early signal on discriminating power before committing further.
- **The ChinaTalk contest's own rubric and deadline were never confirmed.** The contest page could not be fetched from this environment (domain blocked by network policy), and both tracks were scoped from the original announcement text alone. Whether the actual judging criteria reward the depth pursued here over a narrower, faster submission is unverified.
- **Whether under-resisting and regurgitating are one underlying failure or two remains an open, undecided empirical question**, noted in `00-chronicle.md` and repeated in `02-literature-map.md` §4: both a mechanistic-overconfidence finding and the heuristic-rigidity finding point toward a shared low-information-cost-default tendency. Answering this requires data from both tracks, which requires both tracks to exist first, and no plan has been made for how the two would be reconciled statistically (a shared latent-factor or structural-equation model was named as the right tool, not scheduled as work).

## 5. Summary of the genuine contribution, held against the genuine risk

The gap is real. Every existing benchmark surveyed puts a model in the seat of unitary actor, isolated forecaster, or bilateral negotiator, and none tests a model as advisor to a ranked human principal, or tests whether response complexity scales with situational complexity at the moments that matter most. Both tracks target something the field does not yet measure, and both connect cleanly to findings already published under different names, CFPD and Hoover and Payne for the propensity side, the sycophancy literature for the pressure side, shortcut learning and bounded-rationality papers for the regurgitation side. That connective work is itself a contribution independent of any data collected.

Against that, essentially every mechanism proposed to make either benchmark rigorous rather than illustrative, the evidence-strength scale, the Bayesian-game ground truth per scenario, the novelty and complexity operationalization, the QRE fit, the confound controls, is at the stage of being correctly identified as necessary and not yet built. The project's most defensible near-term move, per `03-plumbing-vs-math.md`, is treating track 1 as the more tractable target to actually finish, since it can stay fully black-box, and treating track 2's internal-metrics ambition explicitly as a stretch goal rather than a v1 requirement, so that a defensible result exists even if the mechanistic-interpretability path stalls.
